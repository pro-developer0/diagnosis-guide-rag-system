# Malaria

## Overview
Malaria is a mosquito-borne parasitic infection caused by Plasmodium species. Plasmodium falciparum causes most severe disease.

## Typical presentation
- Fever with chills, sweats, headache, muscle aches, vomiting and fatigue, after exposure in an endemic area.
- Any fever in a person with recent travel to an endemic area should prompt malaria testing.

## Diagnostic criteria
Suspected malaria should be confirmed by a parasitological test, either microscopy or a malaria rapid diagnostic test, before treatment wherever possible.

Features defining severe falciparum malaria include one or more of:
- Impaired consciousness, prostration or multiple convulsions.
- Acidosis or hypoglycemia (blood glucose below 2.2 mmol/L or 40 mg/dL).
- Severe anemia: hemoglobin 5 g/dL or lower in children under 12 years, or 7 g/dL or lower in adults, with parasitemia.
- Renal impairment: creatinine above 265 micromol/L.
- Jaundice with high parasite count.
- Pulmonary edema, significant bleeding or shock.
- Hyperparasitemia: Plasmodium falciparum parasitemia above 10%.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- Thick blood film for detection and thin blood film for species identification and parasite density.
- Malaria rapid diagnostic test.
- If the first test is negative and malaria is still suspected, repeat testing over the next 12 to 24 hours.
- PCR for low-density infection or species confirmation.
- Complete blood count, glucose, renal function, liver function, lactate and blood cultures as indicated.

## Differential diagnosis
- Dengue.
- Typhoid fever.
- Influenza and COVID-19.
- Leptospirosis.
- Sepsis and meningitis.
- Viral hepatitis.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- A single negative test does not exclude malaria.
- Rapid tests based on HRP2 can miss parasites with pfhrp2 deletions, and HRP2 can stay positive after treatment.
- Altered consciousness, repeated convulsions, respiratory distress, shock or severe anemia indicate a medical emergency.

## Guideline basis
World Health Organization guidelines for malaria.
