# Iron Deficiency Anemia

## Overview
Iron deficiency anemia is reduced hemoglobin caused by inadequate iron for red blood cell production, from blood loss, low intake, poor absorption or increased need.

## Typical presentation
- Fatigue, reduced exercise tolerance, breathlessness, palpitations, headache, restless legs and pica.
- Signs: pallor, angular cheilitis, glossitis and koilonychia.
- History should cover menstrual and gastrointestinal blood loss, diet, pregnancy and malabsorption.

## Diagnostic criteria
Anemia by WHO hemoglobin thresholds:
- Adult men: below 13 g/dL.
- Non-pregnant women: below 12 g/dL.
- Pregnancy: below 11 g/dL in the first and third trimesters, and below 10.5 g/dL in the second trimester.

Iron deficiency is shown by low ferritin:
- Ferritin below 30 ng/mL is a widely used threshold in adults.
- Some gastroenterology guidance uses below 45 ng/mL in anemic patients.
- In inflammation or chronic disease, iron deficiency may be present with ferritin up to about 100 ng/mL, particularly with transferrin saturation below 20%.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- Complete blood count: low hemoglobin, usually low MCV and MCH, and raised red cell distribution width.
- Serum ferritin, transferrin saturation, serum iron and total iron-binding capacity.
- Reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear.
- Celiac serology.
- Gastrointestinal evaluation, including bidirectional endoscopy, in men and postmenopausal women without an obvious cause.

## Differential diagnosis
- Thalassemia trait.
- Anemia of chronic inflammation.
- Sideroblastic anemia.
- Lead poisoning.
- Chronic kidney disease.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- Ferritin rises with inflammation, so a normal value does not exclude iron deficiency.
- Iron deficiency can exist before anemia develops, and MCV can be normal early on.
- Unexplained iron deficiency in men and postmenopausal women requires a search for gastrointestinal malignancy.
- Chest pain, syncope, active bleeding or hemodynamic instability need urgent care.

## Guideline basis
World Health Organization guidance on hemoglobin thresholds and ferritin; American Gastroenterological Association guideline on iron deficiency anemia.
