# Community-Acquired Pneumonia

## Overview
Community-acquired pneumonia is an acute infection of the lung parenchyma acquired outside hospital. Common causes include Streptococcus pneumoniae, respiratory viruses, Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae and Legionella.

## Typical presentation
- Fever, cough, sputum, breathlessness and pleuritic chest pain.
- Older adults may present with confusion, falls or functional decline without fever.
- Signs: tachypnea, tachycardia, crackles, bronchial breathing, dullness to percussion.

## Diagnostic criteria
Diagnosis requires compatible acute clinical features together with a new pulmonary infiltrate on chest imaging.

CURB-65 severity score gives 1 point for each of:
- Confusion.
- Urea above 7 mmol/L (blood urea nitrogen above 19 mg/dL).
- Respiratory rate of 30 breaths per minute or higher.
- Systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure of 60 mmHg or lower.
- Age 65 years or older.

A score of 0 to 1 suggests low severity, 2 suggests moderate severity and consideration of admission, and 3 or higher indicates severe pneumonia.

Major criteria for severe pneumonia: septic shock requiring vasopressors, or respiratory failure requiring mechanical ventilation.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- Chest X-ray; CT or lung ultrasound if X-ray is unclear.
- Pulse oximetry, complete blood count, urea, electrolytes and CRP.
- Blood and sputum cultures in severe disease or with risk factors for resistant organisms.
- Pneumococcal and Legionella urinary antigen tests in severe disease.
- Influenza and SARS-CoV-2 testing when these viruses are circulating.

## Differential diagnosis
- Acute bronchitis or viral upper respiratory infection.
- Pulmonary embolism.
- Heart failure with pulmonary edema.
- Exacerbation of COPD or asthma.
- Tuberculosis.
- Lung cancer with post-obstructive infection.
- Organizing pneumonia.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- Early imaging can be normal, especially with dehydration.
- Procalcitonin should not be used alone to decide whether to start antibiotics.
- Non-resolving pneumonia should prompt evaluation for tuberculosis, malignancy or an alternative diagnosis.
- Hypoxemia, shock or altered mental status indicates severe disease.

## Guideline basis
American Thoracic Society and Infectious Diseases Society of America guideline on community-acquired pneumonia; CURB-65 score.
