# Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

## Overview
COPD is a heterogeneous lung condition with chronic respiratory symptoms caused by airway and/or alveolar abnormalities that produce persistent, often progressive, airflow obstruction.

## Typical presentation
- Chronic breathlessness, cough, sputum production and recurrent chest infections.
- Exposure history: tobacco smoke, biomass fuels, occupational dust or chemicals.
- Signs in advanced disease: hyperinflation, reduced breath sounds, pursed-lip breathing.

## Diagnostic criteria
COPD requires compatible symptoms or risk exposure, and spirometry showing a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio below 0.70.

Severity of airflow obstruction by post-bronchodilator FEV1 percent predicted:
- GOLD 1 mild: 80% or higher.
- GOLD 2 moderate: 50 to 79%.
- GOLD 3 severe: 30 to 49%.
- GOLD 4 very severe: below 30%.

Symptom and exacerbation groups:
- Group A: few symptoms and 0 or 1 moderate exacerbations not leading to admission.
- Group B: more symptoms (mMRC 2 or higher, or CAT 10 or higher) and 0 or 1 moderate exacerbations not leading to admission.
- Group E: 2 or more moderate exacerbations, or 1 or more leading to hospitalization.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- Post-bronchodilator spirometry is required for diagnosis.
- Chest imaging to exclude other disease and assess emphysema.
- Alpha-1 antitrypsin testing once in all patients with COPD.
- Blood eosinophil count, complete blood count and pulse oximetry, with arterial blood gases when indicated.
- mMRC dyspnea scale and COPD Assessment Test (CAT).

## Differential diagnosis
- Asthma.
- Heart failure.
- Bronchiectasis.
- Tuberculosis.
- Obliterative bronchiolitis.
- Lung cancer.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- A fixed ratio can over-diagnose older adults, so repeat borderline spirometry and interpret it clinically.
- Hemoptysis, weight loss or a localized chest finding suggests another diagnosis, such as cancer or tuberculosis.
- Acute hypoxemia, hypercapnia, confusion or accessory muscle use indicates a severe exacerbation.

## Guideline basis
Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) report.
