# Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

## Overview
Type 2 diabetes is a chronic metabolic disease marked by persistent hyperglycemia from progressive loss of adequate insulin secretion, usually on a background of insulin resistance.

## Typical presentation
- Often no symptoms; frequently found on screening.
- Polyuria, polydipsia, fatigue, blurred vision, recurrent skin or genital infections, slow wound healing.
- Risk factors: overweight, family history, previous gestational diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, physical inactivity.

## Diagnostic criteria
Any one of the following meets the diagnostic threshold:
- HbA1c at or above 6.5% (48 mmol/mol).
- Fasting plasma glucose at or above 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L), after at least 8 hours without calories.
- 2-hour plasma glucose at or above 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during a 75 g oral glucose tolerance test.
- Random plasma glucose at or above 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) with classic symptoms of hyperglycemia or a hyperglycemic crisis.

Without unequivocal hyperglycemia, diagnosis requires two abnormal results, from the same sample or from two separate samples.

Prediabetes ranges: HbA1c 5.7 to 6.4%, fasting glucose 100 to 125 mg/dL, or 2-hour glucose 140 to 199 mg/dL.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose or oral glucose tolerance test.
- Islet autoantibodies (such as GAD) and C-peptide when type 1 diabetes or latent autoimmune diabetes is suspected.
- At diagnosis: urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, eGFR, lipid profile, retinal examination and foot examination.

## Differential diagnosis
- Type 1 diabetes and latent autoimmune diabetes in adults.
- Monogenic diabetes.
- Pancreatic diabetes, such as after pancreatitis.
- Drug-induced hyperglycemia, such as from glucocorticoids.
- Cushing syndrome and acromegaly.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- HbA1c can be unreliable with hemoglobin variants, pregnancy, recent blood loss or transfusion, erythropoietin therapy and advanced kidney disease. Use plasma glucose in these settings.
- Ketoacidosis, marked dehydration, altered consciousness or very high glucose suggest diabetic ketoacidosis or hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state and need urgent assessment.

## Guideline basis
American Diabetes Association, Standards of Care in Diabetes.
