# Tuberculosis

## Overview
Tuberculosis is an infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, usually affecting the lungs but able to involve almost any organ.

## Typical presentation
- Cough for 2 weeks or longer, fever, night sweats, weight loss and hemoptysis.
- Extrapulmonary disease depends on the site: lymph node enlargement, meningitis, spinal pain, pleural effusion.
- Higher risk with HIV, close contact, diabetes, malnutrition, smoking, immunosuppression and crowded settings.

## Diagnostic criteria
- Bacteriologically confirmed tuberculosis: a positive WHO-recommended rapid molecular diagnostic test, culture or smear from a biological specimen.
- Clinically diagnosed tuberculosis: treatment decided by a clinician without bacteriological confirmation, based on clinical and radiological findings.
- Latent tuberculosis infection: immune evidence of infection (TST or IGRA) with no clinical, microbiological or radiological evidence of active disease.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- WHO-recommended rapid molecular test, such as Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra, as the initial diagnostic test, which also detects rifampicin resistance.
- Mycobacterial culture is the reference standard.
- Sputum smear microscopy where molecular testing is unavailable and for monitoring.
- Drug susceptibility testing for all bacteriologically confirmed cases.
- Chest X-ray for screening and triage.
- Extrapulmonary disease: test samples from the affected site, such as lymph node aspirate, cerebrospinal fluid, pleural fluid or tissue.
- HIV testing for all patients; urine LF-LAM as an additional test for people with HIV.

## Differential diagnosis
- Bacterial pneumonia.
- Non-tuberculous mycobacterial infection.
- Lung cancer and lymphoma.
- Sarcoidosis.
- Fungal infections, including histoplasmosis.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- TST and IGRA do not distinguish latent from active disease, and a negative result does not exclude active tuberculosis.
- A negative smear does not exclude tuberculosis.
- Chest X-ray can be atypical or normal in people with HIV.
- Meningism, massive hemoptysis, spinal cord compression or respiratory failure require urgent care.

## Guideline basis
World Health Organization consolidated guidelines on tuberculosis.
