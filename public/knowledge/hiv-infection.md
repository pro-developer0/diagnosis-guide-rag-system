# HIV Infection

## Overview
HIV is a retrovirus that progressively depletes CD4 T cells. Without treatment, it leads to acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS).

## Typical presentation
- Acute infection: fever, sore throat, rash, swollen lymph nodes, muscle aches and mouth ulcers.
- Chronic infection can be without symptoms for years.
- Advanced disease: opportunistic infections, weight loss and certain cancers.

## Diagnostic criteria
US CDC laboratory algorithm:
- Start with an HIV-1/2 antigen/antibody combination immunoassay.
- If reactive, perform an HIV-1/HIV-2 antibody differentiation assay.
- If the differentiation result is non-reactive or indeterminate, perform an HIV-1 nucleic acid test (NAT).

Infants under 18 months need virological testing (NAT), because maternal antibodies can persist.

AIDS (HIV stage 3) in adults and adolescents: CD4 count below 200 cells per microliter, or an AIDS-defining illness.

WHO testing strategies use three consecutive reactive tests before reporting a positive status in most settings.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- Fourth-generation antigen/antibody test.
- Differentiation assay and HIV-1 RNA for confirmation.
- At diagnosis: CD4 count, viral load, genotypic resistance testing and complete blood count.
- Renal function, liver function and screening for hepatitis B, hepatitis C, syphilis and tuberculosis.

## Differential diagnosis
For acute HIV infection:
- Infectious mononucleosis (Epstein-Barr virus).
- Cytomegalovirus infection.
- Acute viral hepatitis.
- Secondary syphilis.
- Influenza or COVID-19.
- Drug reaction.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- Window periods: NAT can detect infection about 10 to 33 days after exposure, and laboratory antigen/antibody tests about 18 to 45 days after exposure.
- A negative test during the window period does not exclude recent infection, so repeat it and consider NAT when acute infection is suspected.
- Antiretroviral prophylaxis can delay or alter test results.
- Severe breathlessness, meningitis symptoms or focal neurological signs suggest an opportunistic illness.

## Guideline basis
US CDC laboratory testing algorithm and surveillance case definition; World Health Organization HIV testing services guidance.
