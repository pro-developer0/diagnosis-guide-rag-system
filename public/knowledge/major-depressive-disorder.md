# Major Depressive Disorder

## Overview
Major depressive disorder is a mood disorder with persistent low mood or loss of interest, causing significant distress or impaired functioning.

## Typical presentation
- Low mood, loss of pleasure, poor sleep, appetite change, fatigue, poor concentration, guilt, hopelessness.
- May present with unexplained physical symptoms, particularly pain or fatigue.

## Diagnostic criteria
DSM-5-TR requires at least 5 of the following symptoms during the same 2-week period, including at least one of the first two:
- Depressed mood most of the day, nearly every day.
- Markedly reduced interest or pleasure.
- Significant weight or appetite change.
- Insomnia or hypersomnia.
- Psychomotor agitation or retardation observable by others.
- Fatigue or loss of energy.
- Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt.
- Reduced ability to think, concentrate or decide.
- Recurrent thoughts of death, suicidal ideation, plan or attempt.

The symptoms must cause clinically significant distress or impairment and must not be attributable to a substance or another medical condition. There must never have been a manic or hypomanic episode.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- Diagnosis is clinical, by structured interview.
- PHQ-2 and PHQ-9 are screening and severity tools. PHQ-9 severity bands: 5 mild, 10 moderate, 15 moderately severe, 20 severe. A score of 10 or more is a common screening cut-off.
- Suicide risk assessment for every patient.
- Consider TSH, complete blood count, vitamin B12, folate and metabolic panel when clinically indicated.

## Differential diagnosis
- Bipolar disorder.
- Persistent depressive disorder and adjustment disorder.
- Normal grief.
- Substance-induced depressive disorder.
- Hypothyroidism, anemia, sleep apnea, neurocognitive disorder and other medical causes.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- Always screen for past mania or hypomania before concluding major depressive disorder.
- A screening score does not establish the diagnosis without clinical assessment.
- Suicidal intent, psychosis, catatonia or refusal of food and fluids require urgent psychiatric assessment.

## Guideline basis
American Psychiatric Association, DSM-5-TR; Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9).
