# Hepatitis B

## Overview
Hepatitis B is a liver infection caused by the hepatitis B virus, which can be acute or chronic and can lead to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma.

## Typical presentation
- Often no symptoms, particularly in chronic infection.
- Acute illness may cause fatigue, nausea, right upper abdominal pain, dark urine and jaundice.
- Risks: perinatal exposure, unsafe injections, blood exposure, sexual exposure and household contact.

## Diagnostic criteria
- Hepatitis B infection: positive hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg).
- Chronic hepatitis B: HBsAg persisting for at least 6 months.
- Acute hepatitis B: positive HBsAg with positive IgM antibody to core antigen (IgM anti-HBc).

Serology interpretation:
- HBsAg negative, total anti-HBc negative, anti-HBs negative: susceptible.
- HBsAg negative, total anti-HBc negative, anti-HBs positive: immune from vaccination.
- HBsAg negative, total anti-HBc positive, anti-HBs positive: immune from past infection.
- HBsAg positive, total anti-HBc positive, IgM anti-HBc positive, anti-HBs negative: acute infection.
- HBsAg positive, total anti-HBc positive, IgM anti-HBc negative, anti-HBs negative: chronic infection.
- Isolated total anti-HBc positive: past infection, false positive, occult infection or resolving acute infection.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- Triple screening panel: HBsAg, total anti-HBc and anti-HBs.
- HBV DNA quantification, HBeAg and anti-HBe to define the phase of infection.
- ALT, AST, bilirubin, albumin, complete blood count and INR.
- Liver fibrosis assessment by elastography or serum scores.
- Liver ultrasound, and testing for hepatitis D, hepatitis C and HIV.

## Differential diagnosis
- Other viral hepatitis (A, C, D, E).
- Drug-induced liver injury.
- Alcohol-associated hepatitis.
- Autoimmune hepatitis.
- Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- HBsAg can be transiently positive for a short time after vaccination.
- During the acute window period, HBsAg and anti-HBs can both be negative while IgM anti-HBc is positive.
- Normal ALT does not exclude significant liver disease.
- Jaundice with coagulopathy or encephalopathy suggests acute liver failure.

## Guideline basis
World Health Organization hepatitis B guidelines; American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases guidance; US CDC serology interpretation.
