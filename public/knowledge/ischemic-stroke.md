# Acute Ischemic Stroke

## Overview
Acute ischemic stroke is an episode of neurological dysfunction caused by focal infarction of brain, spinal cord or retina. Diagnosis is time-critical because treatment depends on time since onset.

## Typical presentation
- Sudden unilateral face droop, arm or leg weakness or numbness.
- Speech or language disturbance, visual loss, ataxia, vertigo with neurological signs.
- BE-FAST screening prompts: Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time.

## Diagnostic criteria
Diagnosis combines a sudden focal neurological deficit with brain imaging that excludes hemorrhage and, where available, shows acute infarction or an arterial occlusion. A tissue-based definition regards acute infarction on imaging as stroke even when symptoms have resolved.

Record the time the patient was last known well, and assess severity with the NIH Stroke Scale.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- Immediate non-contrast head CT to exclude intracranial hemorrhage.
- Point-of-care blood glucose before treatment decisions, because hypoglycemia can mimic stroke.
- CT angiography for suspected large vessel occlusion; CT or MR perfusion for selected late presenters.
- MRI with diffusion-weighted imaging is the most sensitive test for early infarction.
- ECG, cardiac monitoring, troponin, complete blood count, coagulation studies, renal function and lipid profile.
- Echocardiography and carotid imaging to identify the mechanism.

## Differential diagnosis
- Intracerebral or subarachnoid hemorrhage.
- Hypoglycemia.
- Seizure with post-ictal paralysis.
- Migraine with aura.
- Brain tumor or abscess.
- Functional neurological disorder.
- Transient ischemic attack, when symptoms resolve with no infarction on imaging.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- Early CT can look normal in ischemic stroke.
- Posterior circulation stroke can present as dizziness and be missed.
- Sudden severe headache, reduced consciousness or anticoagulant use raises concern for hemorrhage.

## Guideline basis
American Heart Association and American Stroke Association guidelines for early management of acute ischemic stroke.
