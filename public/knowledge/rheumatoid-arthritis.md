# Rheumatoid Arthritis

## Overview
Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic autoimmune inflammatory disease that mainly affects synovial joints and can cause erosions, disability and extra-articular disease.

## Typical presentation
- Symmetrical pain and swelling of small joints of the hands, wrists and feet.
- Morning stiffness lasting more than 30 to 60 minutes.
- Fatigue and low-grade systemic symptoms.
- Later signs: joint deformity and rheumatoid nodules.

## Diagnostic criteria
The 2010 ACR/EULAR classification criteria apply to a patient with at least one joint with definite clinical synovitis not better explained by another disease. A score of 6 or more out of 10 classifies definite rheumatoid arthritis.

Joint involvement:
- 1 large joint: 0 points.
- 2 to 10 large joints: 1 point.
- 1 to 3 small joints: 2 points.
- 4 to 10 small joints: 3 points.
- More than 10 joints, with at least 1 small joint: 5 points.

Serology:
- Negative rheumatoid factor (RF) and negative anti-CCP (ACPA): 0 points.
- Low-positive RF or ACPA: 2 points.
- High-positive RF or ACPA, above 3 times the upper limit of normal: 3 points.

Acute-phase reactants:
- Normal CRP and normal ESR: 0 points.
- Abnormal CRP or abnormal ESR: 1 point.

Duration of symptoms:
- Less than 6 weeks: 0 points.
- 6 weeks or longer: 1 point.

Patients with erosive disease typical of rheumatoid arthritis can be classified without this score.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- Rheumatoid factor and anti-CCP antibodies.
- CRP and ESR.
- X-rays of the hands and feet.
- Ultrasound or MRI to detect synovitis and early erosions.
- Complete blood count, renal function and liver function.

## Differential diagnosis
- Psoriatic arthritis.
- Systemic lupus erythematosus.
- Osteoarthritis.
- Gout and calcium pyrophosphate deposition disease.
- Viral arthritis, such as from parvovirus B19.
- Reactive arthritis.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- These are classification criteria, so clinical judgment remains essential.
- Negative RF and anti-CCP do not exclude rheumatoid arthritis.
- A single acutely hot, swollen joint with fever suggests septic arthritis and needs urgent aspiration.
- Neck pain with neurological signs suggests atlantoaxial instability.

## Guideline basis
2010 American College of Rheumatology and European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (ACR/EULAR) classification criteria.
