# Heart Failure

## Overview
Heart failure is a clinical syndrome in which a structural or functional cardiac abnormality produces symptoms and signs, with raised natriuretic peptides or objective evidence of congestion.

## Typical presentation
- Exertional breathlessness, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, fatigue, ankle swelling.
- Signs: raised jugular venous pressure, third heart sound, pulmonary crackles, peripheral edema, hepatomegaly.

## Diagnostic criteria
Universal definition: symptoms and/or signs caused by a structural or functional cardiac abnormality, and at least one of:
- Elevated natriuretic peptide levels.
- Objective evidence of pulmonary or systemic congestion.

Natriuretic peptide thresholds supporting heart failure:
- Ambulatory: BNP at or above 35 pg/mL, or NT-proBNP at or above 125 pg/mL.
- Hospitalized or decompensated: BNP at or above 100 pg/mL, or NT-proBNP at or above 300 pg/mL.

Classification by left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF):
- Reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF): LVEF 40% or lower.
- Mildly reduced ejection fraction (HFmrEF): LVEF 41 to 49%.
- Preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF): LVEF 50% or higher, with evidence of structural or functional abnormality or raised filling pressures.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- Transthoracic echocardiography is the key test for structure and LVEF.
- BNP or NT-proBNP, 12-lead ECG and chest X-ray.
- Complete blood count, electrolytes, renal function, liver function, thyroid function, iron studies, glucose or HbA1c and lipid profile.
- Cardiac MRI, ischemia testing or coronary angiography to find the cause.

## Differential diagnosis
- Chronic lung disease or pulmonary embolism.
- Anemia.
- Renal failure or nephrotic syndrome.
- Liver disease or venous insufficiency causing edema.
- Obesity and deconditioning.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- Obesity can lower natriuretic peptide levels.
- Older age, atrial fibrillation and kidney disease can raise them.
- Sacubitril-valsartan raises BNP but not NT-proBNP.
- Cardiogenic shock, acute pulmonary edema or a new arrhythmia need urgent care.

## Guideline basis
Universal Definition and Classification of Heart Failure; American and European heart failure guidelines.
