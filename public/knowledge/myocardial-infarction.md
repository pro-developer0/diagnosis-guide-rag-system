# Acute Myocardial Infarction

## Overview
Acute myocardial infarction is myocardial cell death caused by prolonged ischemia, most often from an atherosclerotic plaque event with coronary thrombosis (type 1). Type 2 results from a supply and demand mismatch.

## Typical presentation
- Chest pressure or pain at rest, often radiating to the arm, neck or jaw, lasting more than a few minutes.
- Breathlessness, sweating, nausea, syncope.
- Atypical or silent presentations are more common in older adults, women and people with diabetes.

## Diagnostic criteria
Acute myocardial infarction requires a rise and/or fall of cardiac troponin with at least one value above the 99th percentile upper reference limit, plus at least one of:
- Symptoms of myocardial ischemia.
- New ischemic ECG changes.
- Development of pathological Q waves.
- Imaging evidence of new loss of viable myocardium or new regional wall motion abnormality.
- Coronary thrombus identified by angiography or autopsy.

ST-elevation criteria: new ST elevation at the J point in 2 contiguous leads of at least 1 mm, except leads V2 to V3, where the threshold is at least 2 mm in men aged 40 or older, at least 2.5 mm in men under 40, and at least 1.5 mm in women.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of first medical contact, repeated if symptoms persist.
- Serial high-sensitivity cardiac troponin, using a validated 0/1-hour, 0/2-hour or 0/3-hour pathway.
- Echocardiography for wall motion and complications.
- Coronary angiography to define the culprit lesion.

## Differential diagnosis
- Aortic dissection.
- Pulmonary embolism.
- Myocarditis and pericarditis.
- Takotsubo cardiomyopathy.
- Pneumothorax.
- Non-ischemic myocardial injury, such as in sepsis or chronic kidney disease.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- A single elevated troponin without a rise or fall suggests chronic myocardial injury rather than acute infarction.
- A normal first ECG does not exclude infarction; repeat it and consider posterior leads.
- Shock, pulmonary edema, new murmur or ventricular arrhythmia suggest a high-risk infarction or mechanical complication.

## Guideline basis
Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction; American and European acute coronary syndrome guidelines.
