# Urinary Tract Infection

## Overview
Urinary tract infection is a bacterial infection of the bladder (cystitis) or kidney (pyelonephritis). Escherichia coli is the most common cause.

## Typical presentation
- Cystitis: painful urination, frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain and sometimes blood in the urine.
- Pyelonephritis: fever, flank pain, costovertebral tenderness, nausea and vomiting.
- Older adults may present atypically, but confusion alone is not enough to diagnose infection.

## Diagnostic criteria
Diagnosis requires compatible urinary symptoms with evidence of infection.
- Uncomplicated cystitis in non-pregnant women can often be diagnosed clinically.
- Urine culture thresholds depend on the setting. A growth of at least 100,000 CFU/mL is the classic threshold, while at least 1,000 CFU/mL may be significant in symptomatic women and in catheter-associated infection.
- Asymptomatic bacteriuria is bacterial growth of at least 100,000 CFU/mL without urinary symptoms, and is not a urinary tract infection.

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- Urinalysis or dipstick for leukocyte esterase, nitrite and blood.
- Urine microscopy for pyuria.
- Midstream urine culture and susceptibility testing for pyelonephritis, men, pregnancy, recurrent infection, treatment failure and complicated infection.
- Blood cultures in sepsis or severe pyelonephritis.
- Renal ultrasound or CT when obstruction, abscess or stones are suspected, or if fever persists.

## Differential diagnosis
- Vaginitis.
- Sexually transmitted urethritis.
- Prostatitis.
- Kidney stones.
- Interstitial cystitis or bladder pain syndrome.
- Appendicitis and pelvic inflammatory disease.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- A negative nitrite does not exclude infection, because Enterococcus and some other organisms do not produce nitrite.
- Contamination is suggested by mixed growth or squamous cells.
- Pyuria or bacteriuria alone, without symptoms, should not be labelled infection.
- Sepsis, obstruction, pregnancy, or persistent fever after 48 to 72 hours of treatment need urgent evaluation.

## Guideline basis
Infectious Diseases Society of America guidance on urinary tract infection and asymptomatic bacteriuria; European Association of Urology guidelines.
