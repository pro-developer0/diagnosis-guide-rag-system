# Chronic Kidney Disease

## Overview
Chronic kidney disease is an abnormality of kidney structure or function that lasts more than 3 months and has implications for health. Diabetes and hypertension are leading causes.

## Typical presentation
- Often no symptoms until advanced disease.
- Later: fatigue, swelling, nausea, itching, reduced urine output, breathlessness and anemia.

## Diagnostic criteria
KDIGO: abnormalities of kidney structure or function present for more than 3 months, shown by either:
- GFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, or
- A marker of kidney damage, such as albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) of 30 mg/g or higher, urine sediment abnormalities, electrolyte abnormalities due to tubular disorders, abnormalities on histology or imaging, or a history of kidney transplant.

GFR categories:
- G1: 90 or higher.
- G2: 60 to 89.
- G3a: 45 to 59.
- G3b: 30 to 44.
- G4: 15 to 29.
- G5: below 15.

Albuminuria categories by ACR:
- A1: below 30 mg/g.
- A2: 30 to 300 mg/g.
- A3: above 300 mg/g.

Classify by Cause, GFR category and Albuminuria category (CGA).

## Confirmatory and supporting tests
- Serum creatinine with eGFR using a validated CKD-EPI equation.
- Cystatin C when creatinine-based eGFR is less reliable, such as in sarcopenia or unusual muscle mass.
- Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and urinalysis with microscopy.
- Kidney ultrasound.
- Electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphate, parathyroid hormone and hemoglobin.
- Autoimmune serology or kidney biopsy when the cause is unclear.

## Differential diagnosis
- Acute kidney injury.
- Pre-renal azotemia.
- Urinary tract obstruction.
- Glomerulonephritis.
- Medication-related kidney injury.

## Diagnostic pitfalls and red flags
- A single abnormal eGFR or ACR is not enough; chronicity must be shown over more than 3 months.
- Creatinine can mislead with low muscle mass, high meat intake or some medications.
- Rapid fall in GFR, hyperkalemia, pulmonary edema, uremic symptoms, or active urine sediment needs urgent evaluation.

## Guideline basis
Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guideline for the evaluation and management of chronic kidney disease.
